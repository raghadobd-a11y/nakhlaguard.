Know the pest

Red Palm Weevil (Rhynchophorus ferrugineus)

The adult is a reddish-brown beetle with a long, curved snout. Its larvae are creamy, legless grubs that feed hidden inside the trunk.

The solution

A simple sensor, a trained ear, a quick decision

NakhlaGuard listens to trunk vibration through a contact sensor, and a machine learning model trained to recognize the acoustic signature of larvae feeding inside the wood. The system classifies severity across four stages and gives an immediate treatment recommendation.

The device

A device on the trunk, a website that analyzes

NakhlaGuard is two parts working together. The device is the source of the readings: it listens to the trunk. The website analyzes what the device sends, shows the result and the 3D position, and alerts the farmer.

🎙️

Sensors on the trunk

🔌

This website

AI result, report, 3D position and alert

The device

  • Listens to the trunk and records 5 seconds at 4,000 readings per second
  • Measures the level on each of the 3 channels, to estimate where the vibration comes from
  • Sends everything to the website automatically, with no file to upload

The website

  • Classifies the sound into four stages: healthy, early, moderate, advanced
  • Writes the treatment report and keeps each palm's history
  • Shows the position on the 3D trunk and alerts the farmer
  • No device at hand? Upload a recording or try a ready sample
Prototype status: the device has not been tested on real infested palms yet, and the position estimate is not calibrated on real wood. See "Not claimed" on the Evidence page

How it works

From vibration to decision, in seconds

01

Listen

A contact sound sensor is mounted on the trunk and wired to a microcontroller (the device).

02

Analyze

An AI model reads the frequency pattern and classifies the palm: healthy, early, moderate, or advanced infestation.

03

Decide

An instant treatment recommendation, with an estimated cost-of-inaction versus cost-of-treatment.

04

Alert

A real-time notification reaches the farmer the moment a reading crosses a risk threshold.

Agentic AI

Ask it anything about your farm — it looks up the real data itself

The Farm Assistant isn't a single canned prompt. It's given real tools — it can query your palm registry, pull one palm's full check history, or list every palm that currently needs attention — and decides on its own, question by question, which tool (if any) to call before answering.

Roadmap

Next: thermal + acoustic fusion

1. Thermal pre-screening

A thermal camera scans the trunk for abnormal heat that fermentation and larval activity can cause. Flagged spots move to the next step.

2. Acoustic confirmation

The contact sound sensor listens at the flagged spot for larval feeding sounds, helping rule out false thermal alarms such as sun-heated bark.

3. Cross-data matching

An algorithm compares both signals. When a heat anomaly and a larval acoustic signature agree, the infestation is marked as confirmed.

4. Locating the larvae

Research direction: 3–4 microphones around the flagged area and time-difference-of-arrival (TDOA) analysis to estimate where the sound originates inside the trunk.

5. Thermal-acoustic farm map

Each palm keeps a thermal and acoustic history on the dashboard, helping follow how infestation may spread between neighboring trees.

6. Targeted treatment (long-term idea)

Using the estimated location to guide localized treatment instead of treating the whole palm. This would need extensive field validation.

Planned research directions — not part of the current prototype and not yet validated.
